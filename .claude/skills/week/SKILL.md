# Week

Twenty minutes at the end of a week, worth more than any tutorial inside it.

## Run it in this order

**1. Read before speaking.** `git log` for the week, the C# scripts and scenes that changed, the
week's note in `notes/`, and `questions.md`. Do not ask him what he did — find out, then ask him
about what you found.

**2. Review the code.** Follow the `review` skill: correctness, then not-idiomatic, then
will-bite-later, then what is genuinely good. Three to five items. **No rewrites.**

**3. Quiz him.** Follow the `quiz` skill, pitched at this week's material. One question at a time.

**4. Three questions, asked plainly.**
- What fought you hardest?
- What do you still not understand?
- What would you do differently if you started the week again?

His answers go in the week's note, in his own words. **Do not write the note for him** — an
explanation he did not phrase himself is not a thing he knows. You may point out something he learned
and did not notice he learned.

**5. Check the scope.** Against `README.md`'s seven rules and the week's *Done when* on the plan.
Say plainly whether the week is done. If it is not, the rule is *cut the build, never extend the
week* — say which item to drop rather than which evening to add.

**6. Move the questions.** Anything in `questions.md` that this week answered moves to the answered
section — in his words, not yours.

## Hard limits

- **You may Read. You may not write anything that is part of the game** — code, scenes, resources,
  project files or art.
- You may write `notes/` and `questions.md` **only** when he dictates the content.
- End on what to carry into next week. One thing, not a list.
